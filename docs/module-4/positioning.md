# Positioning

Positioning defines the place an offer should occupy in the mind of a chosen audience relative to alternatives.

## Positioning choices

A positioning decision should specify the target customer, frame of reference, priority problem, distinctive benefit, reasons to believe, relevant alternative and accepted trade-offs.

## Differentiation

Differentiation must be relevant, credible and difficult to dismiss. It may come from product performance, specialist expertise, service model, access, convenience, trust, community, integration or total cost of ownership.

Claims such as “best quality” or “leading provider” are weak without evidence.

## Positioning statement

> For **[target audience]**, **[brand/offer]** is the **[frame of reference]** that **[primary benefit]** because **[reasons to believe]**, unlike **[alternative]**.

A perceptual map can compare offers on two customer-relevant decision criteria and reveal crowded positions, gaps or unsupported assumptions.
