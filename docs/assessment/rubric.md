# Integrated assessment rubric

| Criterion | Weight | Competent standard | Critical |
|---|---:|---|:---:|
| Market and customer analysis | 12% | Uses relevant evidence, identifies the priority problem and states limitations | Yes |
| Segmentation and targeting | 12% | Defines meaningful segments and justifies a feasible priority target | Yes |
| Value proposition and positioning | 12% | Presents a relevant, differentiated and evidence-supported market position | Yes |
| Marketing mix | 18% | Product, price, place and promotion are coherent, feasible and audience-aligned | Yes |
| Objectives and implementation | 12% | Uses SMART objectives, ownership, timing, dependencies and realistic resources | No |
| Measurement framework | 12% | Defines KPIs, sources, baselines, targets, cadence and decision rules | Yes |
| Ethics, law and risk | 10% | Identifies and controls material customer, privacy, claims and operational risks | Yes |
| Evidence quality | 7% | Evidence is relevant, current, traceable and distinguishes facts from assumptions | Yes |
| Professional communication | 5% | Submission is clear, concise, structured and suitable for decision-makers | No |

## Performance descriptors

### Not yet competent

Evidence is missing, inaccurate, unsupported, internally inconsistent or insufficient for the required decision.

### Competent

Evidence meets the stated requirement, supports a defensible decision and identifies relevant limitations and risks.

### Highly competent

Evidence is integrated, commercially and ethically well judged, explicitly handles uncertainty and demonstrates strong decision discipline.

## Assessment record

The assessor must record criterion marks, critical-criterion decisions, evidence references, verification actions, feedback and the final competence decision.
